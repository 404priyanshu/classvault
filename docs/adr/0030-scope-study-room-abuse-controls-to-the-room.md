# Scope Study-Room Abuse Controls to the Room

For the MVP, ClassVault gives study rooms three abuse controls and keeps every one of them scoped to the room rather than the account. A host or co-host may **remove** a participant from a room they run, which ejects that participant and blocks them from rejoining that room for the remainder of its lifetime; because rooms are temporary and expire on their own, this needs no concept of a permanent ban. A host or co-host may **mute** a participant, which withdraws their ability to post chat messages in that room, again for its remaining lifetime; when camera and microphone arrive, mute extends to withdrawing publish rights server-side rather than asking the client to stop sending. Any member may **report** another participant, and reports go to platform moderators and administrators rather than to the host, because the host is sometimes the problem and because public rooms mix campuses in a way that makes campus-scoped review the wrong boundary. Host controls deliberately do not apply to platform moderators and administrators, so a hostile host cannot eject the person reviewing them. Removal and muting are room-scoped facts with no effect on a participant's account, their notes, or any other room; consequences that follow a student across ClassVault remain account suspension under ADR 0011, which only a platform administrator may apply.

This narrows ADR 0005. That decision made moderation events a deliberate exception to room ephemerality but restricted them to metadata, retaining no chat message content — which would leave a report of abusive chat with nothing for a reviewer to look at, since the room and its history are deleted when it ends. A report may therefore capture the specific messages it cites, up to a bounded number, copied into the report at the moment it is filed. Nothing else about room ephemerality changes: the room, the timer, and the rest of the chat are still deleted when the room ends, reports still retain no video or audio, and a reviewer sees the cited messages and nothing around them. We accept that a reporter chooses what a reviewer sees, because the alternative is either retaining every room's chat or reviewing abuse reports blind.

Abuse limits are server-owned and enforced in the database, not the client: a cap on chat messages per participant per room over a short window, and a cap on rooms created per student per hour. Both are configurable in the same way plan limits already are, so tightening them after the pilot does not require a migration to the application. Removals, mutes, and reports are recorded in an append-only audit trail that names the actor, the target, the room, and the reason, readable by platform moderators and administrators and by nobody else — including the participant who was removed, who is told that a host removed them and not by whom, so that ejection does not become a way to identify who reported you.
